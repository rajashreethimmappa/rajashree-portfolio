How I work

The delivery loop I run on every project, from kick-off to hypercare, whether the team works Agile, Waterfall or hybrid.

  1. Plan with the people who will deliver

    Scope, increments and milestones agreed with engineering, product and QA, with QA in the room from day one.

  2. Make risks and dependencies visible

    One risk register, scored and owned, reviewed weekly with dependent teams.

  3. Report status people can trust

    A few KPIs tracked every sprint, so governance meetings decide instead of discover.

  4. Gate the release

    UAT, integration testing and deployment readiness checked against agreed criteria before anything goes to production.

  5. Support after go-live

    A hypercare period after every release, with production monitored closely and issues routed to the right owner.

  6. Feed lessons back

    Retrospective findings turned into owned improvement actions, raised beyond the team when they affect others.

Four KPIs I track every sprint, each with a target and what I do when it moves. I look at trends over several sprints, never a single number, and I don't use them to compare teams.

Velocity

39 story points, sprint 6

Measures story points completed per sprint.

I act when it swings more than 20% from the 3-sprint average. I forecast with the average, not the best sprint.

Commitment reliability

94% of committed work completed, sprint 6

Measures completed ÷ committed story points.

I act when it drops below 85%: we look at the cause in the retro, usually unplanned work or stories that were too large.

Cycle time

4.5 days median, sprint 6

Measures days from "in progress" to "done" for each item.

I act when it rises above 5 days: I check the board for queues, such as review or UAT waits, and set WIP limits.

Escaped defects

2 defects found after release, sprint 6

Measures defects found in production or UAT after sign-off.

I act when it exceeds 3: each one gets a root cause, and repeated causes become a test or a quality gate.
